# Sleek: a house style for music

**Fits:** the music of product films, demos, launch films and motion-graphics films; any score that should feel sleek,
elegant and contemporary.

The kind of music Ben wants for Ephemeris's demos and product films. Read it when a film's music is one of these,
whether or not the director names it. It does not fit a film whose direction asks for another kind of music (a horror
score, an orchestral score); the director's own direction comes first. It works with the general handbook
(`.agents/skills/sound/MUSIC.md`): the handbook says how to compose, mix and master well; this style says which choices
to make. Read it as intent, not as rules to follow to the letter. Each principle says what it is and why.

The bar: **a sleek product film, not a detergent advert.** Music you would hear in a well-designed store or under a
product reveal: dark, warm, groovy, confident, restrained.

## References

Listen in the direction of these artists. Take their principles, never their material.

- **Sam Gellaitry:** lush, emotional chords; bold, wonky synths; vocal chops; syncopated, glitchy drums; big 808s.
- **Kaytranada:** swung, bouncy grooves; punchy kick and snare; warm Rhodes-type keys and jazzy minor chords; filtered,
  sensual, minimal; percussion.
- **PinkPantheress:** 2-step and garage shuffles, breakbeat energy, soft airy textures, short songs that do one thing.
- **20syl:** precise, crisp hip-hop production; chopped samples; jazz harmony; groove first.
- **CHVRCHES:** synthpop with weight: punchy drums, big analog-style synths, bright melodies over melancholic harmony.
- **Phonk and trap:** 808s that slide, hi-hat triplet rolls, dark minor loops. Use as touches, not as the whole style.
- For clean drop engineering (not for palette): modern Japanese electronic pop such as Pasocom Music Club — a sine kick
  with a fast pitch fall, short sub notes ducked between kicks, a low end with nothing in it but kick and bass.

What they share: dark or bittersweet harmony, groove before everything, a low end that is clean so the kick and bass
hit, warm sounds, space, and restraint.

## 1. Harmony: dark, extended, let lie

- Minor or Dorian by default. Major only with color and a reason (maj9, lydian, borrowed chords).
- Extended chords: m9, m11, maj9 (especially on ♭VI), 6/9, altered dominants (7♭9) for the turn back home.
- Few chords. Two or three can carry a whole film; let them sit and let the energy do the work.
- Voice-led pads that bleed from chord to chord: common tones held, one or two voices moving.
- Resolution saved for the end, or never.

Why: plain major harmony and constant movement made early scores sound "christmassy" and like an advert. Dark,
extended harmony that is allowed to sit reads as confident and elegant.

## 2. Groove: swung, syncopated, with weight

- Swing the sixteenths (about 55–62%). Add small, fixed micro-timing and velocity accents.
- Kick patterns are not always four on the floor: syncopated kicks (1, the "and" of 2, the "and" of 3) bounce. Four on
  the floor is right for house-leaning sections.
- Snare or clap on 2 and 4 with body (a clap layered with a snare), and ghost notes between.
- Hats: swung sixteenths with accents, open hats on off-beats, triplet rolls before transitions.
- Percussion (congas, shakers) for motion where the picture allows; shuffled 2-step patterns as a variation.

Why: groove is what makes this music feel contemporary. Straight, mechanical sixteenths sound like a preset.

## 3. Low end: thumpy kick, clean bass that hits

- The kick is thumpy and clean: a sine falling from about 140 Hz to the key's root in about 50 ms, with a short click
  layer. It is the loudest single element in the drop.
- The bass is an 808 or a clean sub: sine-based, a little second harmonic, light saturation at most ("sometimes
  distorted", but the low end stays clean so the bass hits). 808s slide between notes.
- Kick and 808 often hit together; the 808 ducks briefly under the kick. Or short sub notes sit between kicks.
- Nothing else below about 120 Hz. Mono lows.

Why: muddy low end made the chords impossible to follow and took the punch away. A clean low end is what lets the
kick and bass hit.

## 4. Palette: warm, filtered, human

Use:
- Rhodes-type electric piano, filtered (lowpass around 3.5–5 kHz), for chords and stabs;
- dark, slow pads (saw and triangle, lowpassed around 1.5 kHz, wide), with no sub-octave layer;
- a human voice: sung vowels, chops, phrases; chopped into hooks and fills;
- soft-attack plucks and analog-style leads, filtered;
- risers made of filtered noise, dark rather than hissy.

Avoid:
- bells, glockenspiel, music box, celesta as decoration;
- bright saw stacks and "happy" plucks in major keys;
- anything that collects in 2–8 kHz and sounds tinny;
- untreated presets.

Why: the bright, bell-led palette is what turned a score into a detergent advert. Warm, filtered sounds and a human
voice read as premium.

## 5. Arrangement: simple, with one strong moment

- Short films need one idea and one or two energy changes, not a whole song.
- A melodic rise into the drop (a line climbing by step while the bass holds), a breath just before it (bass and
  harmony out), and the drop on the picture's strongest moment.
- Breakdowns let the picture speak: kick and bass out, the picture's events on soft keys or voice.
- Arpeggios only where they sync to something in the picture.
- Endings fall, breathe, and land.

Why: a drop placed on a camera crash into a new scene was right; music that tried to fit a whole song into twenty
seconds was not.

## 6. Mix and master

- Sidechain everything melodic to the kick: the 808 or bass lightly to deeply (by pattern), pads and keys moderately,
  reverb returns lightly.
- Reverbs and delays on buses, high-passed (about 350 Hz) and lowpassed (about 6 kHz): dark and clean.
- Tonal shape: warm mids, controlled upper mids, a gentle air shelf above 10 kHz. Warmer than a bright pop master,
  never dull: keep presence on the voice and the clap (around 2.5–3 kHz).
- Loudness around −12 LUFS for films and demos (louder only if the brief asks), true peak at or under −1 dBTP.

## Checks

- Would this sell detergent? (Too major, too bright, too bouncy, bells.) Then it is wrong.
- Can you hear the chords change, and do the kick and bass hit?
- Is it swung, with ghost notes and accents?
- Is anything there only as decoration?
